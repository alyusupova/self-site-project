## Week Summary

This week was intense: mathematics, logic, and web development — all mixed together. And you know what? I actually enjoyed it!

### Calculus: Getting to Know Integrals

The biggest discovery — the **definite integral**. Turns out, it's not just a ∫ symbol, but a way to calculate the area under a curve. And it's incredibly beautiful: break a shape into infinitely many tiny rectangles, add them up — and get the exact value.

I learned:
- to understand the geometric meaning of the integral;
- to compute simple integrals using a table of antiderivatives;
- to apply the Newton-Leibniz formula:
  \[
  \int_a^b f(x) \, dx = F(b) - F(a)
  \]
- to solve problems finding the area of a curvilinear trapezoid.

At first, the abundance of symbols was intimidating, but after a dozen examples, I gained confidence. It really helps when the teacher shows not just formulas but also their physical meaning (for example, distance as the integral of velocity).

### Mathematical Logic: Learning to Think Rigorously

Alongside calculus, we delved deeper into **mathematical logic**. This is no longer about numbers but about **truth values of statements**, **logical operations** (conjunction, disjunction, implication, negation), and **quantifiers** (∀ — for all, ∃ — there exists).

I figured out:
- how to build truth tables for complex formulas;
- what tautology and contradiction are;
- how to apply De Morgan's laws to simplify expressions;
- the difference between necessary and sufficient conditions.

At first, implication (`A → B`) seemed weird — why does anything follow from a false premise? But once I understood that in mathematics, the main thing is not to violate truth, it became easier. Now I can analyze problem conditions not intuitively, but with strict logic.

### Web Programming: New Horizons

On the web front, progress too. We started learning **JavaScript** — and it's not just about animating pages, but full-fledged programming in the browser.

What I learned:
- how to work with the DOM (change page content on the fly);
- handle events (clicks, text input);
- use `fetch` for server requests without page reload (AJAX).

I especially liked that you can create an interactive calculator or a form with data validation right in the browser. I wrote a small page that calculates a definite integral using the rectangle method with a button click — and it worked! It feels like you're creating a tiny application without leaving your text editor.

Additionally, I got acquainted with **CSS Grid** and **Flexbox** — now I can build responsive layouts without hacks. Turns out, arranging elements in a row or column can be done with a couple of lines of code, not endless `float` and `position`.

## Impressions

The week was hard but surprisingly coherent. Integrals gave me an understanding of continuous processes, mathematical logic provided a tool for precise reasoning, and web programming offered the ability to immediately see the results of my actions.

The most memorable moment was when during a calculus lecture the professor said: "The integral is the limit of integral sums." And right after, in web development, I implemented that limit numerically — that's the connection between theory and practice!

Among the difficulties: many new symbols and terms (∫, ∀, ∃, λ-expressions in JS...). But by the end of the week, my brain got used to them, and even the integral symbol stopped causing panic.

## Plans for Next Week

- Solidify integrals: learn to compute **improper integrals** and apply variable substitution.
- Understand **Boolean functions** and logic circuit minimization (Karnaugh maps).
- In web: master **React** (they say it will change my perception of frontend development).
- Try to connect mathematics and web — write a small application that draws a function graph and calculates the area under it.

## Conclusion

The fourth week showed that IT is not just code, but also mathematics, logic, and creativity. And the further I go, the more interesting it gets. The main thing is not to be afraid of the new and to solve at least one small problem every day.

See you next week!
